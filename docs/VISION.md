# Product Vision: Astrolab

> **Slogan:** *Spark. Pivot. Build.*  
> **Status:** Strategic Baseline (aligned to PRD v2.0.0)  
> **Target Audience:** Solopreneurs, cross-disciplinary creators, and — further out — company-wide enterprise organizations.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Most ideas die in static notebooks or corporate slide decks. Solopreneurs struggle with blind spots and isolated silos, while organizations reduce creativity to top-down suggestion boxes that disempower frontline employees.

**Astrolab** is an intelligent workspace for ideas. It treats concepts not as dead text files but as structured knowledge units that can be compared, connected, and recombined. Its distinctive act is the **spark**: it deliberately brings together ideas that are *far apart in domain* yet *share a transferable structure*, proposing directions their author would not have reached alone.

The **first release is a personal creative loop** — *Capture → Spark → Fork* — for one creator at a time. The broader ambition (collaboration, enterprise, and prioritization) builds on that loop once it works.

## 2. Core Identity

### Vision Statement
> *To turn static ideas into an evolving body of work that autonomously surfaces unexpected sparks and unites builders across every domain.*

### Mission Statement
> *To turn fragmented ideas into high-impact ventures by autonomously uncovering unexpected cross-domain sparks, guiding smart directions, and equipping creators and teams with the tools to prioritize and build.*

### Slogan
> **Spark. Pivot. Build.**

## 3. The Defining Idea: A Spark Is Distance Plus Alignment

Breakthroughs cluster where distant disciplines meet — but a productive meeting requires two things at once:

| Ingredient | What it provides | Without it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Domain distance** — the ideas come from unrelated fields. | **Surprise.** | Ideas from the same field are the obvious, already-known analogy — *cliché*. |
| **Structural alignment** — the ideas share a transferable *mechanism*, independent of domain. | **Meaning.** | Unrelated ideas with nothing in common are a random pairing — *noise*. |

The value lives in the **intersection**: far apart *and* structurally the same. Distance alone is trivial to produce and worthless; structure alone just surfaces near-duplicates. Astrolab exists to find the far-and-aligned pairs that neither a notes app nor a search box can.

This is why closeness-based tooling — tagging, search, "related items" — cannot deliver the product's value. It optimizes toward the *cliché* quadrant.

## 4. Product Pillars & Capabilities

Each pillar is tagged by phase: **[MVP]** ships first; **[Future]** builds on it.

### Pillar 1: The Idea Vault (Structured Knowledge Units) — **[MVP]**
Ideas are captured with standardized, machine-readable semantics rather than unstructured freeform notes:
* **Core Hypothesis:** The fundamental premise in concise terms.
* **Problem Solved:** Target friction, operational inefficiency, or customer pain point.
* **Monetization & Value Capture:** Revenue model, unit economics, or internal cost savings.
* **Domain Tags:** The axis along which the distance between ideas is measured.
* **Visibility:** Private to the author in the MVP; shared scopes later.

### Pillar 2: The Spark — **[MVP]**
The heart of the product. Operating over a creator's *own* ideas:
* **Internal Sparks:** Scores every pair of ideas on two independent axes — domain distance and structural alignment — and surfaces only those that are both far apart and structurally aligned.
* **Rationale, not magic:** Every surfaced spark explains the shared mechanism and proposes a fork direction.
* **Feedback loop:** Dismissals are remembered so expected non-matches are not re-offered.

### Pillar 3: Living Lineage (Iterations vs. Forks) — **[MVP]**
Ideas evolve like open-source intellectual property:
* **Iterations:** Versioned refinements by the author, kept in place.
* **Forks:** Discrete, brand-new ideas branching off an existing concept into a novel market, audience, or technology stack.
* A fork widens the vault into a new domain — which is what makes the next spark possible.

### Pillar 4: Guided Bootstrap — **[MVP]**
Sparks only pay off once a vault spans several domains. Onboarding therefore *deliberately* elicits the creator's own ideas across unrelated domains, so the product delivers value from the first session rather than after months of accumulation.

### Pillar 5: The Horizon (Prioritization Engine) — **[Future]**
Moving ideas out of the repository and into production:
* **Objective Scoring:** ICE (Impact, Confidence, Ease) and RICE frameworks with customizable weights.
* **Dynamic 2×2 Matrix:** Visual canvas clustering initiatives into Quick Wins, Major Bets, Fill-ins, and Deprioritized tasks.
* **Export to Execution:** One-click handoff to GitHub Issues, Linear, or Jira.

### Pillar 6: Blind Matchmaking & the Shared Graph — **[Future]**
Extending sparks *across* creators — the destination once single-player sparks are proven:
* **Synergy Radar:** Surfaces high-value overlap between different creators' backlogs.
* **Blind Matchmaking Protocol:** Neither party sees raw content until both review a high-level teaser and grant mutual consent.
* **Public Graph:** Opt-in open visibility for global synergy discovery, citation, and forking.

## 5. Phasing at a Glance

| Phase | What ships | Core loop |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **MVP (v1.0)** | Vault, personal lineage (fork/iteration), internal Spark, guided bootstrap. Single user. | *Capture → Spark → Fork* |
| **Future** | Multi-user sharing, blind matchmaking, public graph, enterprise tenancy, and prioritization (*Build*). | *+ Build, + collaborate* |

The MVP deliberately covers only the **creative** loop. Execution and collaboration wait until the creative loop demonstrably produces sparks worth pursuing.

---

## 6. Enterprise & Ecosystem Dynamics — **[Future]**

The following dynamics describe the eventual multi-user destination, not the MVP:
* **Bottom-Up Employee Empowerment:** Any employee can post an idea, fork an initiative from another department, and signal interest to collaborate.
* **Internal Cross-Pollination:** Breaks down departmental silos by connecting operational pain points directly with technical problem-solvers.
* **Incentive Alignment:** Ideas reaching verified priority thresholds trigger discovery sprints, operational budget, and transparent attribution.

---

## 7. Business Model — **[Future]**

Monetization follows the multi-user destination; the MVP is a single-player tool.

* **Free Tier (Community & Exploration):** A capped number of ideas, complete with structured profiles, baseline sparks, and public idea discovery.
* **Flat Subscription (Pro & Enterprise):** Predictable flat pricing unlocking unlimited ideas, private circle workspaces, deep cross-domain spark runs, team collaboration, and dedicated enterprise data isolation.

---

## 8. Strategic Non-Goals

To maintain focus and avoid scope creep, Astrolab will explicitly **not**:

1. **Act as a generic project-management tool:** Astrolab prioritizes *what* to build; execution tracking belongs in Jira, Linear, or GitHub Issues.
2. **Function as an unstructured social feed:** Astrolab requires structured data and validation signals to avoid becoming a repository of low-effort shower thoughts.
3. **Train public models on private user data:** Private entries remain the author's and are air-gapped from shared training corpora.
4. **Optimize for closeness:** The product's value is in *distant-but-aligned* sparks; surfacing near-duplicates and "related items" is the cliché quadrant, not the goal.
